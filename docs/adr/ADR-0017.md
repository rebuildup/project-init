# ADR-0017: Constitutional Organization Model and Refinement-Based Governance

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-09-21
- Extends: ADR-0003, ADR-0005, ADR-0007, ADR-0011, ADR-0012, ADR-0016
- Reclassifies: tool/provider/workflow-specific defaults as operating model or practice unless explicitly promoted to a constitutional invariant
- Superseded by: None

## Context

project-init has accumulated strong policies for isolated execution, immutable delegation, evidence-bound validation, durable recovery, release delivery, review, and explicit merge authorization.

Those policies currently mix different abstraction levels:

- long-lived organizational invariants
- current delivery and planning topology
- current tools and providers
- agent judgment playbooks

This creates two risks.

First, a tool or workflow that is useful today can become indistinguishable from a property that must remain true. A future agent/runtime may provide a safer or simpler mechanism, but a concrete rule can prevent that improvement even when the organizational guarantee would still hold.

Second, semantic-preservation rules make policy growth safer but can make obsolete rules difficult to remove. As agent capability improves, project-init must be able to become smaller without losing organizational correctness.

The desired boundary is therefore not “agents must follow one permanent procedure”. It is “the organization defines a small set of properties that must remain true, while operating models and practices may evolve or be replaced when they refine those properties”.

## Decision

### 1. project-init is an organizational framework

project-init defines the organization in which humans and AI agents develop software.

It does not make a specific agent, IDE, orchestration runtime, hosting provider, planning tool, worktree manager, branching topology, or command surface part of organizational correctness merely because that mechanism is currently preferred.

The canonical hierarchy is:

1. **Constitution** — tool/provider-independent properties that every valid operating model must preserve
2. **Operating Model** — the current organization of roles, planning, delivery, review, release, and authority
3. **Practice** — replaceable mechanisms that implement part of the operating model
4. **Skill** — progressively disclosed judgment/playbooks for work that cannot or should not be reduced to deterministic rules

Higher layers constrain lower layers. Lower layers do not redefine higher-layer correctness.

### 2. Constitutional kernel

The initial constitutional properties are:

#### Identity Integrity

Task, execution attempt, artifact, evidence, decision, and authority identities must not be silently substituted.

Evidence or results that are bound to one immutable identity cannot be treated as evidence or results for another identity without an explicit valid derivation.

#### Authority Integrity

A consequential decision may be committed only by an actor or process that holds the authority for that decision.

Readiness, confidence, successful validation, or possession of technical capability does not by itself create decision authority.

#### Evidence Integrity

An organizational claim must be supported by evidence appropriate to the strength and scope of that claim.

Evidence must remain attributable to the artifact/state that it actually evaluated. Stale, partial, unrelated, or unverifiable evidence must not be represented as current proof.

#### Mutable Ownership Safety

Concurrent work must not create uncoordinated ownership of the same mutable state.

This property may be satisfied through isolation, serialization, transactional coordination, conflict-free semantics, or another mechanism with equivalent or stronger guarantees. A worktree alone is not assumed to prove runtime isolation.

#### Organizational Continuity

Loss of one ephemeral actor, session, process, runtime, or provider must not necessarily destroy the durable organizational state required to understand and safely continue consequential work.

Recovery may use any mechanism that preserves the required durable identities, decisions, evidence, and outstanding obligations.

#### Canonical Consistency

The organization must not intentionally maintain multiple conflicting canonical authorities for the same fact without an explicit reconciliation rule.

Different systems may own different fields or views.

#### Progress

Safety must not be implemented by indefinite inactivity.

When required inputs and authorities are available and no valid blocker remains, work must be able to progress toward an explicit terminal or waiting state. A mechanism that preserves safety only by permanently preventing useful work is not a valid refinement.

### 3. Full-system optimization under constitutional constraints

Agents and humans should optimize for the project's actual objective rather than mechanically maximizing compliance with current practice.

The conceptual model is:

```text
maximize project utility
subject to constitutional invariants
and explicit product / organizational decisions
```

Current operating defaults are evidence-backed starting points, not objectives in themselves.

When a default conflicts with a demonstrably better local approach, the default may be replaced if the alternative preserves the applicable higher-level guarantees and does not override an explicit decision that remains in force.

### 4. Refinement contract

Operating models and practices must state, where material:

- which higher-level property or obligation they implement
- assumptions they require
- guarantees they provide
- evidence used to justify those guarantees
- known limits
- conditions under which deviation is allowed
- re-evaluation and removal triggers

A replacement is valid when it preserves the applicable constitutional and operating obligations.

Formally, for implementation/practice `P` and required invariant set `I`:

```text
P ⊨ I
```

is the intended relationship.

This does not require every practice to carry a machine-checked proof. The strength of evidence should match the risk and the practical ability to formalize the boundary.

### 5. Deviation is a normal path

Deviation from a default practice is not automatically a policy violation.

A deviation is acceptable when:

1. the applicable higher-level obligations are identified
2. the alternative preserves equivalent or stronger guarantees
3. no explicit product/organizational decision forbids the change
4. material uncertainty or risk is surfaced rather than hidden
5. durable evidence is recorded when the deviation changes a consequential organizational boundary

This allows future agent/runtime capabilities to replace current mechanisms without first weakening the Constitution.

### 6. Current release workflow is an operating profile

The current Git/GitHub/Linear/release-branch/Worktrunk workflow remains the standard operating profile for repositories where it fits.

In particular, ADR-0016 remains authoritative for the current release-driven solo-development profile, including:

- Linear release-level planning
- GitHub Issue/PR delivery
- current SemVer intent defaults
- current `main` protection baseline
- release-only main integration
- Worktrunk as the current default WSL/Linux worktree frontend

These are not promoted to constitutional invariants merely by being standard defaults.

Their guarantees must be traceable to higher-level properties such as canonical consistency, authority integrity, evidence integrity, mutable ownership safety, or organizational continuity.

### 7. Formal specification covers the abstract organization

project-init maintains a tool-independent formal model for the constitutional core.

The model describes abstract entities and transitions such as:

- actor
- task
- execution attempt
- artifact
- evidence
- decision
- authority
- mutable resource
- start / delegate / retry / produce / validate / decide / recover / terminate

The initial model is written in TLA+ and checked with TLC over bounded configurations.

Model checking is evidence about the abstract model. It is not represented as a proof that every implementation, Skill, external platform, or real-world execution satisfies the model.

Implementation conformance remains a separate obligation.

### 8. Safety and liveness are both required

The formal model must distinguish:

- **safety** — invalid states/transitions do not occur
- **liveness/progress** — valid work is not permanently prevented from reaching a meaningful terminal/waiting state

A policy change that strengthens safety by making normal work unable to progress is incomplete.

### 9. Policy decay is symmetric with policy preservation

Semantic-loss protection remains required, but non-constitutional rules must also be removable.

A rule may be weakened, moved down a layer, or deleted when evidence shows that:

- its original failure mode is no longer material
- another mechanism now provides the same or stronger guarantee
- the rule duplicates a higher-level property without adding useful operational guidance
- model/tool capability improvements make the instruction unnecessary
- its coordination/context cost exceeds its demonstrated value

Removal is an intentional policy change with the same traceability expectations as addition.

Successful historical behavior alone does not make a rule permanent.

### 10. Evals prioritize outcomes and invariant preservation

Policy evaluation should prefer questions such as:

- did the resulting organization preserve artifact/evidence identity?
- did an unauthorized decision become committed?
- could stale execution results win after retry/recovery?
- could independent actors interfere through mutable state?
- could a fresh actor reconstruct consequential unfinished work?
- did a safer/better alternative get rejected only because it differed from a current tool recipe?
- did added safeguards prevent useful progress?

Tool-name or command-shape assertions are appropriate only when the tool itself is the explicit subject of the practice being evaluated.

## Consequences

### Positive

- future agent/runtime improvements can replace current practices without rewriting organizational correctness
- existing strong recovery, evidence, authority, and isolation rules gain a smaller common foundation
- tool-specific Skills remain useful without becoming permanent organizational constraints
- policy growth gains a symmetric removal mechanism
- formal modeling can find concurrency/recovery/identity counterexamples at the organizational layer
- project-init can become simpler as model capability improves

### Negative / Trade-offs

- current rules must be reclassified and some documents will need substantial restructuring
- “default” and “invariant” must be distinguished carefully in reviews
- refinement evidence is sometimes qualitative rather than machine-provable
- formal verification covers an abstraction and does not eliminate implementation/testing/review
- agents need enough context to discover the current operating profile after the root contract becomes smaller

## Re-evaluation triggers

Re-evaluate this decision when:

- constitutional properties prove too weak to exclude a recurring organizational failure
- a property is so implementation-specific that it cannot survive major tool/provider changes
- formal modeling repeatedly fails to provide useful counterexamples or clarification
- the operating/profile hierarchy adds more coordination cost than it removes
- substantially stronger agents make current Skills or operating profiles unnecessary
